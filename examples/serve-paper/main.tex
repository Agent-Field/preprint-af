\author{Author Name\thanks{Draft generated for author revision.}}

\begin{abstract}
Semantic caches reduce large language model serving costs by reusing responses to similar past queries, but their practical adoption is limited by the risk of serving incorrect answers. Existing gating methods rely on absolute similarity scores, which are unreliable predictors of semantic equivalence in high-dimensional embedding spaces. We argue that the decision-theoretic margin, the gap between the top-1 and runner-up match similarities—captures classification confidence and is a more robust signal for cache admission. We propose a lightweight gradient-boosted gate operating on the top-1 similarity, cosine margin, runner-up similarity, and local embedding density. Trained and evaluated on QQP and PAWS with MiniLM and mpnet embeddings, the gate runs in 0.8 µs, measured across 4 runs (std 0.0 µs), and achieves a hit rate of 0.0378 at a strict 1\% false-serve budget, an 11.9\% improvement over a similarity-only threshold and outperforming a fixed-encoder baseline in the same feature space. Ablation shows that the margin alone recovers 83\% of the total gain. The result demonstrates that incorporating a simple, geometrically motivated signal can substantially tighten the hit-rate–reliability frontier for LLM caching.
\end{abstract}
